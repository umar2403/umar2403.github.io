about me

About me

I'm a graduate student at UC San Diego, specialising in Intelligent Systems, Robotics, and Controls. At the Advanced Robotics and Controls Lab (ARC Lab) I work on sim-to-real reinforcement learning for dexterous grasping in clutter, training a multi-fingered hand in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and transferring the learned policies onto real hardware. At the Existential Robotics Lab (ERL) I currently work on primitive latent-skill vision-language-action (VLA) models that compose reusable skills to complete multi-step tasks, having previously developed object-level SLAM that represents scenes as 3D Gaussian splats inside a Ceres factor-graph backend.

I completed my B.Tech in Electrical Engineering (Minor in Computing) at IIT Madras, where I worked on reinforcement learning and control, including a project on Q-learning with function approximation for large state-action spaces, benchmarked against dynamic programming.

In summer 2024 I worked as a Software Developer Intern at DentalPaaS, where I created and optimised backend routes for extracting data from the database and for operations.

My core interests are in robot learning for dexterous manipulation, spanning reinforcement learning, vision-language-action models, SLAM, and perception, with a focus on sim-to-real transfer onto real robotic hardware.

Graduate Researcher

Gaussian Splatting Object SLAM
Existential Robotics Lab (ERL) · Prof. Nikolay Atanasov · UC San Diego
  • Developed an online object-level SLAM system on KITTI that represents scene objects as 3D Gaussian splats, pairing real-time reconstruction with factor-graph localization.
  • Integrated Gaussian-splat object models into a Ceres factor-graph backend, using differentiable rendering to constrain camera and object poses via photometric consistency rather than geometric feature matches.
  • Designed a retroactive factor-insertion framework that links newly reconstructed objects to historical keyframes for global trajectory refinement — without explicit loop-closure detection.
